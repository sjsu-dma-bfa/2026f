SYDNEY ETO

Holographic Reliquaries: Fragmentation and Reconstruction of Identity

Artist Statement / Bio

My artistic practice explores how human identity, technological systems, and social environments shape human experience. In my work, I use digital media, sculpture, 3D modeling, animation, and interactive technologies to explore how contemporary technologies influence human perception, behavior, and interactions. A central theme of my work is the relationship between individuality and standardization. I am interested in how the documentation, classification, and representation of human experience by digital systems create new ways of constructing and interpreting identity. The translation of subjective experience into standardized information is a key issue that I explore, and the extent to which that information is preserved or altered in its representation. I use digital manipulation, simulation, and re-contextualization of objects and cultural structures in my work to explore the space between physical and virtual, authentic and constructed, human agency and technology. My work does not attempt to create a positive or negative view of technology. Rather, it attempts to explore the contradictions inherent in technological systems and to invite the viewer to consider how we are shaped by these systems and how we in turn shape them. Artwork Statement

Holographic Reliquaries: The Fragmentation and Reconstruction of Identity is a mixed-media installation with three sculptures incorporating holographic illusions and digital animation. Inspired by traditional religious reliquaries, the installation explores the way technological systems are preserving and reconstructing aspects of the human identity. The three sculptures in the installation explore different aspects of human experience: Avatar (digital classification and identity construction), Intimacy (mediation and simulation of interpersonal relationships), and Memory (digitization and selective preservation of personal experiences). The three works explore the ways in which digital representations preserve or transform the complexity of human experience through processes of abstraction, fragmentation, and reconstruction.
