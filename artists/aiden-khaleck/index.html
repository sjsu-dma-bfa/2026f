AIDEN KHALECK

Ofrenda de Luz

Artist Statement / Bio

In a time when Mexican and Mexican-American communities can feel pressured to hide, assimilate, or make themselves smaller, Aiden Khaleck created Ofrenda de Luz as a symbol of visibility, resilience, and cultural pride. The illuminated skull stands as a beacon, representing the strength of communities that continue to preserve their identity, traditions, and language despite displacement, discrimination, and social pressure. Drawing from Día de los Muertos traditions, the light within the skull references candles placed on ofrendas to honor and guide loved ones who have passed. Decorative cutouts, words, and cultural imagery allow that light to spread throughout the surrounding space, reflecting how memory, stories, and identity are carried across generations. The work is also deeply personal to Khaleck, the son of a Lao father and Mexican mother who both immigrated to the United States. Their sacrifice, determination, and hard work created opportunities that shaped his life. Ofrenda de Luz honors not only his Mexican heritage, but immigrant communities more broadly, celebrating those whose resilience creates possibilities for future generations. The work reminds viewers that culture is not left behind; it is carried forward, protected, transformed, and kept alive.

Artwork Statement

Ofrenda de Luz is a sculptural installation that transforms the skull into a symbol of remembrance, resilience, and cultural presence. Inspired by Día de los Muertos, the illuminated sculpture references candles placed on ofrendas to honor and guide loved ones who have passed. Light travels through cutout words, symbols, and imagery, projecting them into the surrounding space. These projections represent how memory, language, and identity move across generations. Rather than symbolizing death alone, the skull becomes a beacon of continuity, honoring those who came before while celebrating the strength of those who carry their culture forward.
